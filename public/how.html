How it works یہ کیسے کام کرتا ہے؟

Every loan app charges in its own way. This page explains each formula in plain words, with one worked example per loan — so you can check the calculator's numbers by hand.

1. Easypaisa Easycash (App) — weekly, compounded

Each week the app adds a percentage of what you still owe — and the next week's percentage is charged on the bigger amount, because the fee joins the loan. That is compounding. A part-week counts daily at the weekly rate ÷ 7.

fee each week = what you owe × rate  ·  rate = 5% (cycles 1–30), 4.5% (31–60), 4% (61+)  ·  fee stops at 40% of the loan (8 weeks)

Worked example: Rs 10,000 for 4 weeks at 5% → 10,000 × 1.05 × 1.05 × 1.05 × 1.05 = Rs 12,155. You pay Rs 2,155 extra.

There is no late fee on the app route — the weekly charge itself is the cost of keeping the loan longer. Markup is charged per day of use.

2. Easypaisa Easycash (USSD) — flat fee for 30 days

The fee is one flat percentage of the loan for the whole 30-day term: 20% (cycles 1–30), 18% (31–60), 16% (61+). It applies in full for the term. If you are more than 2 days past the due date, one extra 5% of the loan is added once.

total = loan × (1 + flat %)  ·  late (once, after 2 grace days) = loan × 5%

Worked example: Rs 10,000, cycles 1–30, repaid on day 30 → 10,000 × 1.20 = Rs 12,000. Repaid on day 35 (past the grace) → 12,000 + 500 = Rs 12,500.

3. Easypaisa Buy Now Pay Later — flat fee per term

10% for 14 days, or 20% for 30 days. One late fee of 5% of the loan, charged from the first day past the due date (no grace period is stated).

total = loan × (1 + 10% or 20%)  ·  late (once, past the due date) = loan × 5%

Worked example: Rs 10,000 for 30 days → 10,000 × 1.20 = Rs 12,000. One day late → 12,000 + 500 = Rs 12,500.

4. JazzCash ReadyCash — 5% a week, simple

Each week adds 5% of the original loan — the fee does not compound. That is why Rs 100 becomes 105, 110, 115, 120 over weeks 1–4. The fee stops at 40% (8 weeks / 56 days).

fee = loan × 5% × weeks  ·  due 28 days, max 56 days, cap 40%

Worked example: Rs 5,000 for 1 week → 5,000 × 1.05 = Rs 5,250. Rs 5,000 for 4 weeks → 5,000 × 1.20 = Rs 6,000.

There is no separate late fee — the weekly fee itself is the cost of being late. Partial repayment lowers the fee, because it then runs on what is left. This calculator assumes you repay the whole loan at once.

5. Daraz PayLater — flat fee, in instalments

16% for a 1-month plan (1 instalment), 30% for a 3-month plan (3 monthly instalments of 10% each). The late fee is charged per overdue instalment: 5% of that instalment, capped at Rs 800.

total = price × (1 + 16% or 30%)  ·  instalment = total ÷ 1 or 3  ·  late per overdue instalment = min(5% × instalment, Rs 800)

Worked example: Rs 20,000 on the 3-month plan → total Rs 26,000, instalment Rs 8,667. One instalment late → 5% × 8,667 ≈ Rs 433 (under the Rs 800 cap).

6. JazzCash BNPL — flat fee, 3 instalments

15% service fee on the price, repaid in 3 instalments. The late fee is 10% of each overdue instalment.

total = price × 1.15  ·  instalment = total ÷ 3  ·  late per overdue instalment = 10% × instalment

Worked example: Rs 10,000 → total Rs 11,500, instalment Rs 3,833. One instalment late → 10% × 3,833 ≈ Rs 383.

Verified against the JazzCash Yeylo page and its merchant terms (Nov 2026): this is JazzCash's BNPL product ("Yeylo"), 15% service fee, 3 instalments at 0 / 30 / 60 days, late fee 10% of the overdue instalment, early repayment free.

7. Custom loan

For any other app or lender. Pick the fee type — flat % for the whole term, % per week simple, or % per week compounded — then the rate, the term, the late-fee type, the grace days, and a cap. The calculator applies exactly what you typed, and says so.

8. Bank and microfinance personal loans — EMI on a reducing balance

A bank loan is repaid in equal monthly instalments (EMI). Each instalment pays that month's interest first, and the rest reduces the loan — so the interest part shrinks every month. The rate is fixed for the whole loan, or it is 1-year KIBOR plus a spread (see section 9).

EMI = loan × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1)  ·  r = annual rate ÷ 12  ·  n = months

Worked example: Rs 100,000 at 12% a year for 12 months → instalment Rs 8,885, total Rs 106,619, interest Rs 6,619. The same loan at HBL's 35.99% costs Rs 10,046 a month, Rs 120,549 in total.

The calculator assumes you pay every instalment on its due date. The planned repayment date is the day you settle the loan — settle early and you pay the remaining balance (before any early-settlement fee the bank charges separately). A late fee only counts if you pay after the loan's end date.

9. KIBOR + spread — the rate moves with the market

Most bank loans are priced as 1-year KIBOR plus a spread. KIBOR is the rate banks charge each other, published daily by the State Bank — about 12.8% in late October 2026. The spread is the bank's margin, fixed in your Key Fact Statement (or set per customer, as with JS CashAsaan).

annual rate = 1-year KIBOR + spread  ·  then the EMI formula of section 8

Worked example: MCB Personal Loan, qualifying credit history: 12.8% KIBOR + 14% = 26.8% a year. Rs 100,000 for 12 months → instalment Rs 9,592, total Rs 115,104. Non-qualifying (+16% = 28.8%) → instalment Rs 9,690, total Rs 116,277.

KIBOR changes daily — the calculator uses 12.8% (late Oct 2026) unless you type today's rate. Always check today's KIBOR before you borrow.

10. Islamic financing — a fixed sale price, not interest

In Musawamah the bank buys an asset and sells it to you at an agreed, fixed total price, paid in instalments. The profit rate only sets that price — once set, it does not compound like interest. A late payment cannot become the bank's income, so it goes to charity.

total = loan × (1 + profit rate × years)  ·  instalment = total ÷ months

Worked example: Meezan Islamic Personal Finance, Rs 100,000 at 16.55% for 24 months → profit 100,000 × 16.55% × 2 = Rs 33,100, total Rs 133,100, instalment Rs 5,546.

11. Buy Now Pay Later — profit built into the price

With QistBazaar you pay part of the price first (30% down), and the rest plus a fixed profit in instalments. With Baadmay a 15% processing fee is spread across 3 instalments. There is no interest rate to compound — the total is fixed on day one.

total = down + financed × (1 + markup %)  ·  instalment = financed × (1 + markup %) ÷ months

Worked example: QistBazaar, Rs 100,000 phone, 3 months: Rs 30,000 down, financed Rs 70,000 + 8% = Rs 75,600, so 3 instalments of Rs 25,200 and Rs 105,600 paid in total. Baadmay: Rs 10,000 → total Rs 11,500, instalment Rs 3,833.

12. Salary advance — one flat fee, repaid next payday

Earned-wage access is not a loan: you draw part of the salary you have already earned, pay one flat fee, and it is taken from your next salary. There is no interest and no late fee.

total = amount drawn + flat fee

Worked example: Abhi, Rs 10,000 drawn → fee Rs 150 → Rs 10,150 repaid from your next salary. (The fee is reported as Rs 100 in older guides and Rs 150 in 2025–26 guides — unverified; the calculator uses Rs 150, the higher figure.)

13. "Rate on request" — when the calculator will not guess

Some lenders publish no rate at all (UBL CashPlus, Standard Chartered, NBP Advance Salary — where the figures even conflict). For those the calculator shows no number: it tells you to ask for the Key Fact Statement and use Custom loan with the rate the bank quotes you. A guessed rate would be worse than no rate.

Licensed nano loan apps

The SECP whitelist (29 Sep 2026) lists 17 licensed nano loan apps. Their rates are shown in-app only, so the calculator lists them — with their licensed NBFC — on the calculator page, with the SECP guardrails: at most Rs 25,000 per app and Rs 75,000 across all apps, tenure at most 90 days, total cost at most the loan itself, APR at most 10× the SBP policy rate (120% a year at the current 12%). Whether those caps survived the Nov 2025 amendments is unconfirmed — the calculator says so on screen. Any app NOT on the whitelist is unauthorised: Barwaqt was delisted in Aug 2024, QisstPay's app was removed in Jul 2024, and KalPay is not whitelisted. Checked against the 11 Sep 2026 edition: the 17 nano apps are identical; the only addition is Bunyaad Finance (Credsys, B2B financing, not a consumer loan).

What "effective cost" and "annualised" mean

Effective cost % = (what you pay back − what you borrowed) ÷ what you borrowed. A Rs 20,000 loan repaid as Rs 24,000 costs 20%.

Annualised cost = the same cost stretched to a year, by simple proportion (cost % × 365 ÷ days). It is an approximation, shown so you can compare a 28-day loan with a bank loan — it is not the lender's APR.

Remember: these loans are expensive. A "small" 5% a week is 20% in a month and 40% in two months. Borrow only what you can repay on time, and check the rate inside the app before you borrow — rates change every quarter.
